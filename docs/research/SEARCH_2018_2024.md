# The search on 2018-2024: protocol and log

**Pre-registered 2026-10-24, before any of 2018-2024 was read.** The commit that adds this file fixes the protocol and the first hypotheses. Later hypotheses are appended with their own dates, before they are run.

## Why

Every direction source tried on January 2025 to June 2026 has come out at chance:
- the engine's setups and every filter on them;
- the trader's trend counted five ways, ranked swings and breaks;
- the dominant trend;
- the draw on liquidity.

Those 18 months have been read so many times that anything found there could be luck. The trader (2026-10-24): keep working until there is an edge, without the blind sessions.

The broker's terminal holds D1, H4, H1 and M15 back to mid-2018 for the nine focus markets (DXY from 2019). `scripts/export_mt5_history.py` wrote them to `data/research/snapshots/mt5-2018-2026`. No research has read 2018-2024.

## The protocol

- **Development:** 2018-07-01 to 2023-01-01. Every hypothesis is run here first, on the nine markets, against its pass rule.
- **Test:** 2023-01-01 to 2025-01-01. Nothing is read here until a hypothesis passes development. Each one that does gets one run. With k hypotheses reaching the test together, each must clear a (1 - 0.05/k) interval (Holm-style), so luck across candidates is paid for.
- **2025-2026:** a third look only, since it has been studied.
- **The log:** every hypothesis run on development is listed below with its result, passes and failures alike. That is the count of tries.
- **A hypothesis that passes both** becomes a trade design: entry, stop, target and costs, measured in R. It is then registered again, for the paper runners going forward.

The price path is M15. At the daily scale an M15 bar rarely reaches both a target and a stop, so outcomes settle on M15 bars, with the stop winning a bar that does both.

### Amendment 1 (2026-10-24, before any outcome was read)

The bars' spacing shows that the server holds no intraday history before 16 July 2021. Before that date, its H4, H1 and M15 (and M1) are the D1 bars, one per day, stamped at the lower timeframe. So the M15 path above doesn't exist for 2018 to mid-2021. This was found before any swing or outcome had been computed. The export now drops the stand-in rows and records each market's first intraday date.

- **D1-scale hypotheses (H-A to H-C) read the broker's D1 bars as their price path, in every window**, so development and test use the same rule:
  - the bar is the server day (00:00 UTC). Sunday's short session (about a fifth of a day's range) is merged into Monday;
  - W1 bars are built from them. Swings, the previous week's extreme, the W1 objectives and the outcomes are all read on D1 bars;
  - a D1 bar that trades beyond both the objective and the extreme counts as failed (the stop wins, as the atlas rules a bar that does both). Their number is reported;
  - the day ends at 00:00 UTC, two to three hours after the engine's 17:00 New York close. A real D1 effect should survive that shift.
- **Reported beside the pass rule, not part of it:**
  - lows and highs apart: an excess that comes from one side only is the markets' drift, not the swing;
  - an interval that resamples calendar months, since D1 outcomes run for weeks and neighbouring dates overlap.
- **Intraday hypotheses** develop on 2021-07-16 to 2023-01-01 and are tested on 2023-2024, the same test window.

### Added 2026-10-24, before they were run: daily direction calls (H-D to H-G)

The engine's entries pay when the day's direction is known. Task 338: the indicator's setups made +0.29 to +0.37R with the day's real direction, and a bias right 56-61% of the time clears costs. So a source that calls the D1 candle better than the market's own drift is the missing piece. These four are documented effects, with priors from the literature, not from this data.

- **The call:** made at a D1 close, for the next D1 candle. Up means it closes above its open.
- **Hit rate against the base rate:** the share of calls the next candle confirms, minus the share of that market's D1 candles in the window that went the call's way (its drift). A candle that closes at its open counts for neither.
- **Pass on development:** the excess is above zero, its 95% trading-date interval is above zero, and it is positive in at least two-thirds of the markets it covers (6 of 9; 4 of the 5 equity indices).
- **Companions, not part of the rule:**
  - the absolute hit rate, against the engine's 56-61%;
  - the next candle's body in units of the 20-day average range, signed by the call, minus the market's mean body signed the same way.
- **Terms:** the 200-day average is the mean of the last 200 closes, today's included, so calls that use it start after 200 bars. A 5-day closing low is a close below each of the previous four closes, and a 5-day closing high the mirror. The equity indices are US100, US500, US30, GER40 and UK100.

**H-D. A 5-day closing low above the 200-day average calls the next equity-index day up.** Prior: the dip-buying rules documented on US index futures (Connors and Alvarez), and daily reversal in equity indices.

**H-E. Short-term reversal, all nine markets:** a 5-day closing low calls the next day up, and a 5-day closing high calls it down.

**H-F. The 200-day trend, all nine markets:** a close above the 200-day average calls the next day up, and a close below it calls it down. Prior: time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012), documented over months; daily, it is weak.

**H-G. Turn of the month, equity indices:** the month's last trading day and its first three are called up. Prior: Lakonishok and Smidt (1988), McConnell and Xu (2008).

### Added 2026-10-24, before they were run: intraday effects (H-H, H-I)

These are on the M15 bars, so they develop on 2021-07-16 to 2023-01-01 (Amendment 1). Times are each index's own cash session in its local time:
- US100, US500, US30: 09:30-16:00 New York;
- GER40: 09:00-17:30 Frankfurt;
- UK100: 08:00-16:30 London.

Prices are bid. A buy pays the bar's spread, or the market's median spread if larger.

**H-H. The opening-range breakout, five indices.** Prior: Zarattini and Aziz (2023) on QQQ, with a 5-minute range; M15 is the finest bar these years hold.
- **The range** is the session's first M15 bar. A bar closing above its open is bought at the next bar's open, with the stop at the range's low; a bar closing below its open is sold, with the stop at its high. A range that closes at its open is not traded.
- **The exit** is the stop, or the cash close (the last bar's close). A bar that opens beyond the stop exits at its open.
- A trade whose risk is under two spreads is skipped.
- **Pass on development:** the mean R net of the spread is above zero, its 95% trading-date interval is above zero, and it is positive in at least 4 of the 5 indices.

**H-I. Intraday momentum, US indices.** Prior: Gao, Han, Li and Zhou (2018, JFE), on SPY.
- **The call:** the move from the previous cash close (16:00) to 10:00 calls the last half-hour (15:30-16:00) its way.
- **Judged like the daily calls:** the hit rate minus the share of that index's last half-hours that went the call's way.
- **Pass on development:** the excess is above zero, its 95% trading-date interval is above zero, and it is positive in at least 2 of the 3 indices.

### Added 2026-10-24, before it was run: ICT's top-down chain (H-K)

Every layer of ICT's bias hierarchy has been measured alone on 2025-2026, and none carried direction:
- the draw on liquidity;
- premium and discount;
- the previous candle's close;
- structure and the trend;
- delivery phases and time.

The hierarchy itself was never tested. H-K reads the layers together, top-down, as the context-engine spec's chain (Requirements 3, 4, 27 and 28; tasks 277, 278, 365 and 366). Its call is a daily direction call, so it is judged as H-D to H-G are. The trader confirmed the pass rule on 2026-10-24, before the run.

**H-K. ICT's chain, MN1 → W1 → D1, calls the next D1 candle.** `python -m algo_research.search_chain --window develop`.
- **The frames:** all three are built from the broker's D1 bars (Amendment 1).
  - A week closes with its Friday bar and a month with its last weekday's. One whose last trading day falls earlier closes when the next one opens.
  - The forming week and month stretch their frame's dealing range.
- **Each frame's state** (`liquidity_engine/context/frame_state.py`):
  - **the dealing range:** from the last confirmed swing low to the last confirmed swing high (two candles each side);
  - **the zone:** premium above 55%, discount below 45%;
  - **the objectives above and below:** untaken swings; the previous candle's high and low on the frame and on those above it; unfilled gaps at their near edge; and the IPDA ranges, the 20, 40 and 60-day highs and lows;
  - **the trend:** the last closed candle closed beyond the previous one's range;
  - **its own direction:** trending, toward the nearest objective that way; otherwise toward the nearer objective;
  - **its POIs:** the nearest order block, breaker or gap each way, in the matching zone, and not traded into before the range formed.
- **The chain (`PD_POI`):**
  - MN1 takes its own direction.
  - Each lower frame goes with the frame above it, except when price sits in that frame's wrong zone (premium for a bullish frame, discount for a bearish one) with its POI unreached. Then it goes the other way, toward the POI.
  - A neutral frame makes every frame below it neutral.
- **The call:** at each D1 close, the D1 link's direction, for the next D1 candle. There is no call while the chain is neutral or has no draw, or while any frame lacks a dealing range.
- **Pass on development:** the same rule as H-D to H-G.
  - The hit rate minus the market's base rate is above zero.
  - Its 95% trading-date interval is above zero.
  - It is positive in at least 6 of the 9 markets.
- **Companions, not part of the rule:**
  - the absolute hit rate, against the 56-61% the engine's executions need;
  - the body, and an interval over resampled months;
  - the calls by case (with the parent, toward its POI);
  - the draw reached before the D1 objective on the other side, against a driftless walk's chance;
  - the ablations: `PROFILE`, the monthly link dropped, no IPDA ranges, and each frame's own direction alone.

  An ablation that beats H-K is a lead derived from development data, not a pass.
- **If it passes,** it gets one run on 2023-2024, at a 95% interval, or at the Holm level if other hypotheses reach the test with it.

### Added 2026-10-24, after H-K's run and before its own: SMT at a major array (H-L)

The trader's rule (2026-10-24): SMT divergence is a confluence tool, not a standalone signal. Look for it only where price meets a major institutional array: a higher-timeframe order block or breaker, a major liquidity pool, or the premium or discount extreme of a dealing range. The trader confirmed the definition and the pass rule before the run.

**H-L. SMT at a major array calls the next D1 candle.** `python -m algo_research.search_smt --window develop`.
- **The pairs**, on the same server date: EURUSD-GBPUSD; US100-US500-US30, each pair; and the dollar index inverse to EURUSD, GBPUSD and gold. GER40 and UK100 have no partner here.
- **Bullish SMT:** one leg trades below its previous day's low while its partner holds above its own. The dollar index, inverse, holds below its previous day's high instead. Bearish SMT is the mirror.
- **A leg is called**, for the next D1 candle and on its side of the divergence, only when its own day is at a major array. Any one of these counts:
  1. it traded into an unfilled D1 order block or breaker of that side;
  2. it took a major pool on the other side: the previous week's extreme, an untaken D1 swing (two candles each side), or the 20-day extreme (the 40 and 60-day extremes lie beyond it);
  3. it closed in the outer 25% of its W1 dealing range (the bottom for bullish, the top for bearish).

  A market called both ways on one day is not called. The arrays are D1 only, because the broker holds no H4 history before July 2021.
- **Pass on development:** the daily calls' rule, over the seven paired markets.
  - The hit rate minus the market's base rate is above zero.
  - Its 95% trading-date interval is above zero.
  - It is positive in at least 5 of the 7 markets.
- **Companions, not part of the rule:**
  - SMT with no gate, and each gate alone;
  - the leg that took its level against the one that held;
  - the calls H-K agreed with, which shows whether SMT adds to the chain;
  - the absolute hit rate, against 56-61%.
- **If it passes,** one run on 2023-2024, at the Holm level with any other hypothesis that reaches the test.

### Added 2026-10-24, after H-L's run and before its own: ICT's confluences stacked (H-M)

The trader (2026-10-24): test everything as a whole, not each part apart, stacking confluences to arrive at a conclusion. H-K chained the frames along one path. H-M is a vote: every read speaks, and a call is made only when enough of them agree. It asks whether ICT's reads, each near zero alone, add up when they line up.

Every component has already been read on this window, alone or in H-K and H-L, and each was within a point or two of drift. H-M is therefore a new try on data whose parts are known. The votes, their weights and the threshold were fixed without looking at how they combine. The trader confirmed the design and the pass rule before the run.

**H-M. Thirteen ICT confluences, stacked, call the next D1 candle.** `python -m algo_research.search_stack --window develop`.
- **The votes,** each +1 (bullish), −1 (bearish) or 0 (abstain), with equal weight (nothing is fitted; AR-D10):
  1. MN1's own direction (H-K's frame state);
  2. W1's own direction;
  3. D1's own direction;
  4. the chain's call (H-K);
  5. the candle profile (`PROFILE`);
  6. W1 discount (+1) or premium (−1);
  7. D1 discount (+1) or premium (−1);
  8. structure, on D1;
  9. consecutive closes;
  10. the failed sweep;
  11. arrays respected or traded through;
  12. PO3;
  13. SMT on the previous day's high and low, ungated, for the paired markets; the others abstain.

  Votes 8 to 12 are the trader's five trend signals (`algo_research.trend`).
- **The score** is the sum of the votes, from −13 to +13.
- **The call:** the score's sign when it is +7 or more, or −7 or less, while every frame has a dealing range. That is a clear majority of all thirteen, on balance.
- **Pass on development:** the daily calls' rule.
  - The hit rate minus the market's base rate is above zero.
  - Its 95% trading-date interval is above zero.
  - It is positive in at least 6 of the 9 markets.
- **Companions, not part of the rule:**
  - the dose-response: the excess at each level of agreement, |score| 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 and 7+;
  - the votes' mean pairwise correlation, which shows how much of the stack is the same read repeated;
  - the share of closes at each level;
  - the absolute hit rate, against 56-61%.
- **If it passes,** one run on 2023-2024, at the Holm level with any other hypothesis that reaches the test.

### Added 2026-10-27, before it was run: the trader's M15 CRT, entered at market on M1, managed with the partial (H-N)

The trader's design (2026-10-27): "with the M15 CRT model, market entries on the 1 minutes and targetting 3, 5 or 5 r but we use the same approach, Partials and BE at 3". Read as targets of 3, 4 and 5R, the partial being the backtester's (Requirement 28: a third off at 3R, the rest's stop to the fill).

**What is known already** (the 2025-2026 slices, so this window is fresh for it):
- The mechanical CRT on M15 (2026-10-09) entered at C3's open, stopped at C2's extreme and targeting C1's far side was about 0R gross on every frame, −0.29R net on M15.
- An M1 sweep → CISD raced to a fixed 5R (2026-10-23) did no better than its mirror.

H-N differs from both in its exits: fixed multiples of R and the partial.

**The rule:**
- **Data:** M1 bars (bid) exported from the terminal for this window only (`mt5-m1-develop`, 2021-07-16 to 2023-01-01, `scripts/export_mt5_history.py --timeframes M1`); M15 from this search's snapshot. The nine markets.
- **The CRT:** C1 and C2 are consecutive M15 bars.
  - A short: C2 trades above C1's high, not below its low, and closes below C1's high.
  - A long: the mirror.
  - A C2 that takes both sides is not traded.
- **Entry:** at market at C3's open, the first M1 bar of C3. A buy pays the ask (the open plus the spread); a sell takes the bid. No entry when C3 opens from 16:00 to 18:00 New York (the day's last hour and the rollover).
- **Stop:** C2's extreme. As in H-H, the short's stop sits a spread above the high and triggers when the bid trades through it. A trade whose risk is under two spreads is skipped.
- **Exits:** three targets, each read as its own trade:
  - 3R;
  - 4R with the partial: a third off at 3R, the rest's stop to the fill from the next bar;
  - 5R with the same partial.

  A bar reaching the stop and the partial or target is the stop's. A bar opening beyond the stop exits at its open. Whatever is open at the trading day's close (17:00 New York) exits at that bar's close.
- **Costs:** the bar's recorded spread, or the market's median M1 spread in the window when larger. No slippage. No swap: trades close by the day's end.
- **Every CRT is a trade.** There's no one-at-a-time rule: the per-trade mean is judged. The interval resamples trading dates, so trades of one day move together.
- **Pass on development:** for a target, the mean R net is above zero, its trading-date interval is above zero at the Holm level for three targets (98.3%), and it is positive in at least 6 of the 9 markets. H-N passes if any of its targets does. It then gets one run on 2023-2024, at the Holm level with any other hypothesis that reaches the test.
- **Reported beside it, not judged:**
  - gross R;
  - the mirror: the same moment and risk, the other side;
  - trades with risk of at least 10 spreads;
  - the London (02:00–05:00) and New York AM (07:00–10:00) killzones;
  - how often 3R is reached.

### Added 2026-10-27, after H-O's development run: the quarterly theory's false move, traded into Q3 (H-P)

The trader (2026-10-27): "Quarterly theory: in every candle there is going to be a phase of calm (accumulation), then a false move, followed by an expansion. Two of those phases, especially Q2 and Q3, is where most of the volatility is."

**This one is derived from development data:** it was written after H-N and H-O, and it carries H-O's gap lead. Passing development therefore proves little, and the test window is its real trial.

- **Day:** Q1 18:00–00:00, Q2 00:00–06:00, Q3 06:00–12:00, Q4 12:00–18:00.
- **Session:** each of those six hours cut into four quarters of 90 minutes.

Within a set of quarters (a day, or one six-hour session), Q1 is C1, Q2 is C2 and Q3 is C3. The quarters are larger than H-N's M15 candles, so the stop sits more spreads away.

**The rule:**
- **The false move:** Q2 trades beyond one side of Q1's range, not the other, and closes back inside it.
- **The trade:** at market at Q3's open (its first M1 bar), against the false move. The stop is beyond Q2's extreme, with H-N's rule for the short's spread.
  - The target is 5R, a third off at 3R and the rest's stop to the fill.
  - The trade exits by the trading day's close (17:00 New York).
  - H-N's costs and two-spread floor apply.
- **At a gap:** Q2's wick reached into an unfilled H1 or H4 fair value gap of the trade's side, formed before Q2 opened, and Q2 closes short of its far edge (H-O's definition).
- **Four cells:**
  - day quarters;
  - day quarters at a gap;
  - session quarters;
  - session quarters at a gap.
- **Pass on development:** for a cell, the mean R net is above zero, its trading-date interval at the Holm level for four cells (98.75%) is above zero, and it is positive in at least 6 of the 9 markets.
- **Reported beside it, not judged:**
  - the mirror;
  - the median risk in spreads;
  - each quarter's share of its set's range (day and session), which tests the claim that Q2 and Q3 carry most of the volatility.
